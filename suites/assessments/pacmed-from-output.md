# PACMED 12 km pyesper run: assessment from the restart scan

Source: `phase5/pacmed-scan/` only (78 restarts, 2010-01-02 to 2013-12-31; 1,209,079 ocean rho cells x 100 levels per file). Scan columns follow `scan_restarts.py`. Snapshots are last records of each restart, level means are area-weighted per s-level, and inventories include negative values.

## 1. Summary

The run is numerically stable: no NaN or Inf in any variable, volume is conserved to 0.0015%, and the physical and carbon-chemistry state variables (salt, ALK, DIC, O2) are nearly conserved. There are no visible discontinuities at task boundaries or at the 2010 to 2011 continuation. The biogeochemistry is the weak point. The plankton and DOM pools were initialized inconsistently (phytoplankton P is 10 to 30 times too high relative to C) and spin up violently over the first 2 to 4 weeks. DOC, DON and DOP are negative in about a third of all cells from the first file onward and keep decaying (about 19% over 4 years). Plankton pools turn negative in over half of cells by 2013, though almost entirely at depth and at tiny magnitudes. The oxygen minimum zones are eroding steadily: suboxic volume (O2 < 20 mmol/m3) is down 44%, and the loss accelerates rather than leveling off. Deep water warms about 0.012 degC/yr and loses oxygen near the seabed. pH is unusable in 0.2% of cells and the abiotic pH diagnostic is zero everywhere. Extremes sit overwhelmingly in 15 m, near-land river-mouth cells, with a few suspicious open-ocean spikes (temperature -5.3 degC, sea level +/-5 m in the Philippines).

## 3. Most important issues, ranked

1. Plankton and DOM pools are inconsistent from the start and spin up for weeks; plankton P is 10 to 30 times too high relative to C and phytoplankton drift continues through 2013 (13, 12).
2. OMZs are filling in: suboxic volume is down 44%, accelerating, with O2 up at 100 to 540 m (15).
3. DOC, DON and DOP are negative in about a third of cells from day 2 and keep decaying about 5%/yr (1, 12).
4. pH is 0 in 2,596 cells at all levels and the abiotic pH field is all zeros; mask or fix before using pH (4).
5. Deep water warms and loses oxygen at the seabed far faster than observed (16).
6. River-mouth cells carry extreme, inconsistent BGC loads: ALK 43 to 5541, DOC to 1074 mmol/m3 (5).
7. Physical spikes: temp -5.3 degC off Sanriku, zeta +/-5 m in the Philippine seas, speeds over 3 m/s (7, 8, 9).
8. Widespread small negatives in NO3, NH4, Fe and the deep plankton pools show positivity is not enforced (2, 3).

## 4. What this scan cannot tell

- Spatial structure beyond the single extreme location per variable per file. Hotspots of negatives are not mapped, and river-forcing versus advection ringing cannot be separated.
- Time resolution: only 78 end-of-record snapshots, mostly monthly, so sub-monthly jumps at task starts are not resolvable (the "at task boundary" tags in summary.txt coincide with multi-week gaps and are not evidence of jumps).
- Whether tides and sea ice are on, atmospheric forcing, or the boundary file contents (ESPER) versus the interior.
- True vertical structure: level means mix columns of different depths (s-coordinates), and band levels in the 290-cell north band are not 200 m or 1000 m, so the summary "band minus interior" extremes mostly compare shelf with deep water.
- Fluxes (air-sea CO2, remineralization, ventilation), which are needed to attribute drift causes.
- Observational realism (no comparison to WOA, GLODAP, SeaWiFS, etc.).
